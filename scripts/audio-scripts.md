# Audio scripts — ready for ElevenLabs

> Generated from `src/data` (`npm run docs`). Use an **adult** voice (contest rule: no voices of people under 18).
> Export as MP3, ~30–60 s, and save to `public/audio/{id}_{lang}.mp3`. The site falls back to the browser voice when a file is missing.
> Optional per-part clips: `public/audio/parts/{vehicle}_{part}_{lang}.mp3` (text = the "Simple" explanation).

## Lunar Roving Vehicle — EN  →  `public/audio/lrv_en.mp3`

_106 words_

Once upon a time, a little car travelled to the Moon, folded up like a camping chair. Two astronauts unfolded it, sat down, and drove across the grey dust, farther than their boots could ever carry them. The car had wire wheels that could never go flat, and a camera that sent live pictures back to Earth. Its batteries could not be recharged, and the rocket home could only carry people and precious rocks. So, when the last drive was over, the car was parked. Its camera watched its friends fly away. It is still there today, waiting in the silence, next to its own tracks.

## Opportunity — EN  →  `public/audio/opportunity_en.mp3`

_116 words_

Once upon a time, a small robot bounced onto Mars wrapped in airbags, and rolled into a tiny crater. Its name was Opportunity. It was supposed to work for ninety days. Every morning, it opened its solar wings to the Sun and went looking for clues. It found little grey spheres, nicknamed blueberries, that whispered a secret: long ago, there was water here. It got stuck in the sand, and its friends on Earth set it free. It drove more than a marathon. Then, one June, a dust storm covered the whole sky. The light faded, and Opportunity went to sleep. It is still there, on the edge of a great crater, dreaming of the Sun.

## Pioneer 10 — EN  →  `public/audio/pioneer10_en.mp3`

_115 words_

Once upon a time, in 1972, a small probe with a big ear left Earth. Her name was Pioneer 10. She was supposed to travel for twenty-one months. She crossed the asteroid belt, where no one had gone before, and flew past giant Jupiter, taking its very first close-up portraits. She had no solar panels, because so far from the Sun the light is too weak. Instead, she carried warm nuclear hearts. Year after year, she whispered to Earth, a little more softly each time. After thirty-one years, her voice faded away. But she is still travelling, toward a red star called Aldebaran, carrying a drawing of us, and a map to find our home.
